**PROPOSED, not normative.** Drafted 2026-11-18 by a writing-only lane (no builds, no runs) while stage H closes. | KR-5 | Incremental curve update | A sequential bootstrap recomputes only pillars at or after the first changed quote. Pillars before it are copied, not recomputed, so they are bit-identical to the previous curve. | KR-6 | Trigger rule | A recalibration trigger fires when `|x_now - x_ref| > k sigma sqrt(d)`, strictly greater; `sigma` is the sample standard deviation (divisor n - 1) of the supplied window of past one-day changes, `d` the number of days since the last calibration. `k <= 0` or `d < 1` is `E_DOMAIN`; fewer than two changes is `E_INPUT`; `sigma = 0` is `E_DOMAIN` (no noise scale). The stated level is the two-sided normal false-alarm rate `2(1 - Phi(k))`, reported as an output |
| KR-7 | Intraday error bound | Intraday risk is a delta-gamma estimate with a Lagrange remainder bound: `|V(x + h) - V(x) - V' h - V'' h^2 / 2| <= |h|^3 M3 / 6`, `M3` an upper bound on `|V'''|` over the move. Wave 1 ships the closed form for zero-coupon positions (`M3 = T^3 N exp(-T min(y, y + h))`); other instruments supply `M3` from their own spec or are not covered by the bound. A reported number without its bound is not "intraday risk with error bounds" |
| KR-8 | Bermudan ladder (K-8, R6-27) | The fix is a new method, not a change to G-18. `CN_RANNACHER_SMOOTHED`: Crank-Nicolson with Rannacher start-up (two fully implicit half steps after each exercise date and at maturity), and the exercise `max` applied as a cell average over each node's cell instead of pointwise. | KR-9 | Richardson guard | Richardson extrapolation is applied only when three grids refined by the same ratio `r` give an observed order within 0.5 of the assumed order and successive differences of the same sign. The R6 grid file shows why: several ladder entries oscillate as the grid is refined, and extrapolating an oscillation gives a confident wrong number |

## K-8c follow-up rulings (2026-11-19), written before the second acceptance run

The first acceptance run of `CN_RANNACHER_SMOOTHED` (`oracle/K8_ACCEPTANCE_W1.json`) failed acceptance 3 on 18 entries. The cause was found by separate refinement in time and in space (`program/K8_DIAGNOSIS.md`, measured). The two rulings below change the method and the reference grid. They do not change the 1% threshold, the default grid 201/100, the three judged cases, the order window 1.5 to 2.5 or the rule that picks the judged entries. Both were committed before any acceptance run of the revised method.

| # | Point | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| KR-15 | `CN_RANNACHER_SMOOTHED`, revised definition | Three changes to the KR-8 method, each with its measured cause and its source. (a) **Curve term integrated exactly.** The scheme solves for `U(t,x) = V(t,x) exp(-int_0^t alpha)`, which removes `alpha(t)` from the operator; the curve enters only through `P(0,t)` at exercise and payment times. Cause: with `alpha` sampled at step ends, a curve node inside a time step (here 0.4986 years) gives a first-order error in the two neighbouring ladder entries (measured: time-only refinement at 401 nodes, order 1.00 on entries 1 and 2, 2.00 on every entry after the change). Source: the factor is the exact solution of the reaction term, which commutes with the rest of the operator (derived here; inferred). (b) **Exercise smoothing by a hat-weighted average with an end correction**, replacing the cell average. Node `i` takes `E_i` plus the average of `max(V - E, 0)` against the hat function on `[x_(i-1), x_(i+1)]`, `V - E` linear between nodes, plus a third-order correction in the cell that holds the crossing, followed by the stencil `1 - delta^2/6` so that nodes away from the crossing keep `max(V, E)`. Cause: a ladder entry is an exercise probability, so its data are a step, not a kink. A cell average is a first-order smoothing operator; for step data a second-order scheme needs a second-order one, whose transform vanishes to second order at the grid frequencies: the hat function [Kreiss, Thomee, Widlund, "Smoothing of initial data and rates of convergence for parabolic difference equations", *Comm. Pure Appl. Math.* 23 (1970) 241-259 (recalled, not read); Pooley, Vetzal, Forsyth 2003, sections 2.2 and 2.4, where the cell average is credited to that paper and the remark is made that averaged data may still have kinks (read 2026-11-19, https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~paforsyt/report.pdf)]. The end correction removes the remaining term that depends on where the crossing sits in its cell (derived here; measured: space-only refinement on seven grids, error times `(nx-1)^2` constant to 2% with it, varying by a factor of 4 without it). (c) **Four implicit half steps** (the first two steps) after maturity and after each exercise date instead of two [Giles and Carter 2006, section 1 and Table 1: four half steps are the minimum that keeps the high-wavenumber error at or below second order for the value and its first two derivatives of both a call and a digital (read 2026-11-19, https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/gilesm/files/giles_carter.pdf)]. The formulas are in `06-risk/bermudan-ladder-k8.md` version 0.2.0. The method name is kept: no golden of the first definition was ever published |
| KR-16 | K-8 reference grid | Acceptance 1 proposed QuantLib `tGrid` 2,000 and `xGrid` 801. QuantLib's engine applies the exercise `max` pointwise, so its bumped derivative carries the same grid-position noise this item is about. The reference is therefore taken at `tGrid` 8,000 and `xGrid` 3,201 (finer, never coarser), with the same two bumps (1e-4 and 5e-5 relative, central) and the same 0.1% agreement rule; an entry that fails the rule is reported as unresolved, as before. A bump-size study (eleven bumps from 1e-5 to 1.6e-2) is recorded next to the reference to show the plateau. The proposed 2,000/801 reference is also run and recorded, not used to judge. The choice of grid uses only QuantLib's own behaviour (plateau, agreement between its two grids), never the comparison with the method under test |
