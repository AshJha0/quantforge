**Label on every row:** "fitted to public US Treasury par yields with a library Nelson-Siegel fit; not an official curve". The comparison with the Federal Reserve's curve is labelled **"consistency check, not proof"**.

**Plain idea.** Each US business day, after the Treasury posts its par yield curve, a small job on a free GitHub runner downloads that file, fits a three-factor Nelson-Siegel curve to it with the library's own bond fit, and writes one row: the fitted parameters, how far the fitted curve is from each published yield, the SHA-256 of what it downloaded, and the code commit. (Parameters and distances together give the published yields back; see "What is fetched and what is published".) Each row carries the hash of the row before it, and the head is stamped outside the repository, so an old row cannot be changed quietly. Days that go wrong are written down as going wrong (a gap, a failed fit, a file that had not been updated yet), never left out. When the Fed later posts its own fitted curve for the same day, a second row compares the two at 1 to 30 years.

**Worked example (measured, local laptop, not the acceptance machine; dry run, nothing pushed).** The very first live dry run (13:36:05 UTC on 2026-11-19) used an unweighted price fit and gave RMSE 31.58 bp, largest error 70.58 bp at 1 month: with equal weights the bills' tiny price errors let their yields drift. The weights `1/MD^2` of KR-98 were added after that one real day was seen. That is a method change made after seeing data, and it is recorded here; the thresholds did not change. The two times are 42 seconds apart, and they are the lane's own session record, not a log kept in the repository: what the repository shows is the tools committed unweighted at 13:31:21 UTC (7440c936) and the weights committed at 13:38:10 UTC (eff76a68, a change of 14 lines in the core file, read 2026-11-19 from `git log`). Whether the weighted variant had been drafted before the first run was looked at is not recorded, and this spec does not claim either way; the disclosure does not depend on it, because the weights were adopted with one real day in view. Run again at 13:36:47 UTC with `--now` unset, the job found 2026-11-18 due, fetched the Treasury file for the year and the Fed file, and wrote (in dry-run output only) a GENESIS row and one DAY row: `ok`, `on_time`, tau 2.25 years, RMSE 10.58 bp, largest error 17.91 bp at 1 month, so `within_fit_flag` "false" (the flag is 10 bp RMSE). The Fed file then ended at 2026-11-13, so the comparison stayed pending. A second dry run with `--now 2026-11-13T23:45:00Z` fitted 2026-11-13 (tau 1.5, RMSE 10.25 bp, largest 15.91 bp at 20 years) and compared it with the Fed's zero yields: RMSE 17.36 bp, largest 25.71 bp at 22 years, status `outside` the 15 bp RMSE tolerance. Both results stand as measured; the thresholds were committed (commit 7440c936, `tools/curve_daily/config.json`) before either run and are not moved. The wave in the differences (about +18 bp at 7 years, about -26 bp at 22 years) is the shape a three-factor curve cannot follow on a curve with two bends; see Limits.

**What is fetched and what is published** (KR-209, one position for this job and the paper ledger).

Plain idea: the job keeps no copy of either source's file, but its rows do let a reader work the published numbers back out, and this spec says so instead of claiming otherwise.

Formal statement.
1. No source file and no source row is stored, committed, uploaded or printed by the scheduled job. Downloads stay in the runner's memory. A row holds the file's SHA-256 and size, and a SHA-256 of the day's thirteen values as one text line.
2. A DAY row holds `betas`, `tau` and `errors_bp`. `errors_bp` is, per tenor, fitted yield minus published yield at full binary64 precision. Anyone can reprice the thirteen par bonds on the stored parameters and take the errors off, which gives the thirteen published par yields back to the last digit. **The rows therefore disclose the thirteen Treasury par yields used.** Version 0.1.0 of this spec said that rows hold "no source row" and that the job "publishes less than" the recorder; that was wrong and is withdrawn (audit KA-C11).
3. A CONSISTENCY row holds `diffs_bp`, our zero rate minus the Fed's, beside the DAY row's `zeros_pct`. The Fed's `SVENY` values compared (up to thirty) follow by subtraction.
4. Basis for the Treasury values: the recorder's rule and its reading of this source. `docs/developer-guides/recorder-actions.md` says forward-log rows carry, "for public-domain sources only, the parsed values"; `tools/recorder/sources.json` classes this file as a US federal government work [17USC105] with `redistribute_raw` "believed yes, unconfirmed" and notes that Treasury's own terms were not found [UST-POL]. Read 2026-11-19 on `origin/forward-log`: the recorder publishes parsed values of three Treasury series on that reading (five par yields, three bill rates, two real yields). The precedent is the reading of the source, and the file is the same one here. The precedent's own rows hold five par tenors, not thirteen, and the reading is unconfirmed; both are stated here plainly.
5. Basis for the Fed values: the Board's disclaimer puts its material in the public domain unless otherwise indicated and asks to be cited as the source [FRB-DIS]. The citation is in every CONSISTENCY row (`gsw.attribution`), in the GENESIS row and in the README of branch `curve-daily`.
6. Citations: each record carries the attribution of the source whose data it uses, the GENESIS row holds both (KR-109), and the branch README names both in the form each asks for. A record that used no source data (a failed fetch, a gap closed by the late window) carries a URL and no attribution.
7. No page read permits or forbids automated fetching for either source; both offer machine-readable downloads ([UST-XML], [FRB-NYC]).
8. If the recorder's reading of the Treasury source changes, this ledger and the paper ledger stop publishing recoverable values under new format strings; rows already published are not rewritten.

Worked example: a row with `tau` 2.25, three betas and `errors_bp["10 Yr"]` = 3.2 says the fitted 10 year par yield was 3.2 bp above the published one. Price the 10 year par bond on the stored curve, turn the price into a yield, subtract 3.2 bp: that is the published 10 year yield. The rebuild check (`verify_chain.py rebuild`) is this arithmetic run the other way.

This is a reading of published terms, not legal advice.

## Inputs (KR-91, KR-92)

- **Treasury par yields** for business day `D`: the CSV for `D`'s year [UST-CSV]. Thirteen tenors are used, fixed in `tools/curve_daily/config.json`: 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 months; 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 20 and 30 years. Any other column (for example a 1.5 month column) is ignored. Values are percent, two decimals, as published. A missing or unreadable cell for one of the thirteen, in a row that is present for the date, is a failed fit (`E_MISSING_TENOR`, `E_PARSE`), not a smaller fit. A download that is not the file at all (no `Date` column, a date that does not read, not UTF-8, empty: for example an error page served with HTTP 200) is a failed fetch and is retried (KR-204).
- **Fed GSW file** [FRB-NYC]: columns `SVENY01` to `SVENY30` (continuously compounded zero yields, percent). Read only for the consistency rows.
- **Prereg holdout windows**: `research/holdout_scan.py` `windows()`, the same reader that `research/test_preregs.py` uses.
- **Calendar**: library calendar `USGS` through `core.calendar.is_business_day` (KR-93), as the paper ledger. Early closes are business days.

## The fit (KR-97 to KR-100)

Plain idea: treat each published par yield as a bond that trades at par, and let the library fit a Nelson-Siegel curve to those bond prices.

Formal statement, for business day `D` (settlement `D`; T+1 settlement is not modelled):

1. For each tenor `k`, a par bond `{maturity: D + k months (day clamped to month end), coupon: y_k / 100, frequency: 2}`. `bond.price.from_yield` at yield `y_k / 100` gives `dirty_k`, `accrued_k` and modified duration `MD_k`. For tenors of 6 months and more `dirty_k` is 100; below 6 months the bond has one cash flow and the operation's ACT/ACT ICMA accrual makes `dirty_k` slightly different from 100.
2. Weights `w_k = 1 / MD_k^2`, so the operation's price objective `sum w_k (model_k - dirty_k)^2` is close to the sum of squared yield errors.
3. `bond.fit.nelson_siegel` with these bonds and weights, once for each `tau` in the grid 0.25, 0.5, ..., 10 years (40 values, the H-20 A3 grid [H20]). The chosen `tau` has the lowest objective; ties go to the smaller `tau`. A `tau` whose call fails is listed with its error code; if every `tau` fails, the day is `fit_failed` with the first code.
4. Zero rates: the same operation at the chosen `tau`, with 30 zero-coupon probe bonds (maturity `D + n` years, `n = 1..30`, coupon 0, dirty price 100) added at weight 0. A weight-0 bond does not move the fit (`bonds.md`: "a bond with weight 0 shows its richness"), and its residual is model price minus 100. The job checks that the betas and the 13 residuals are bit-identical to step 3 (else `E_PROBE_MISMATCH`). `z_n = -ln((100 + r_n) / 100) / t_n`, `t_n = days / 365` as in `bonds.md`, in percent: a unit change, not a model.
5. Fit errors: each par bond's model clean price (`dirty_k + residual_k - accrued_k`) goes back through `bond.yield.from_price`; `error_k = (yield_k - y_k / 100) x 10^4` bp. `rmse_bp = sqrt(mean(error_k^2))`, `max_abs_bp` the largest `|error_k|` and `max_at` its tenor (first one on a tie).
6. **Fit flag** (informational, not a gate): `within_fit_flag` is "true" when `rmse_bp <= 10` and `max_abs_bp <= 25`. Reason, fixed before any real-day run: the same three-factor model fitted to the Fed's own smooth curve had a 95th-percentile RMSE of 9.21 bp and a maximum of 19.99 bp [H20], so a daily RMSE above 10 bp is in the tail of what model shape alone gave there; 25 bp is about 2.5 times that RMSE for a single point. Evidence class: inferred from measured.

The job adds no numerical method of its own. Its own arithmetic is: the weights in step 2, the price-to-rate change in step 4, the clean price in step 5, the bp change, the RMSE and the maximum.

**Worked example (synthetic, from the tests).** A day with par yields from a smooth curve (two decimals) fits with 40 of 40 `tau` values succeeding and an RMSE of a few bp; `python/tests/test_curve_daily.py` asserts RMSE below 10 bp for 30 such days.

## Consistency with the Fed's curve (KR-101)

Plain idea: once the Fed posts its fitted curve for the same day, compare the two zero curves at whole years. Agreement shows the job, the conventions and the data agree with an independent fit; disagreement shows by how much they differ. It is a **consistency check, not proof**: neither curve is the truth, they are fitted to different bonds by different models.

Formal statement. For an `ok` DAY row of date `D` and the Fed row of date `D`: `diff_n = (z_n - SVENYn) x 100` bp for `n = 1..30` where the Fed value is present. `rmse_bp`, `max_abs_bp`, `max_at` as above. Status `within` when `rmse_bp <= 15` and `max_abs_bp <= 40`, else `outside`.

**Tolerance and its reason, fixed before any real-day run** (committed in `config.json` at 7440c936 before the first live dry run): three effects add up. (a) Model shape: a three-factor fit to the Fed's own four-factor curve leaves a median 3.81 bp, 95th percentile 9.21 bp and maximum 19.99 bp RMSE [H20], measured. (b) Different inputs: the Treasury par curve rests on indicative quotes for recent issues and bills [UST-IRS], the Fed curve on off-the-run notes and bonds without bills [GSW07]; a few bp, more at the long end, inferred. (c) Par to zero through the fitted model, sub-bp, inferred. 15 bp RMSE allows (a) at its 95th percentile plus (b); 40 bp for one point is twice the largest (a) seen. `outside` is a result, not a fit failure, and is never re-labelled. A day outside the tolerance is written as a `CONSISTENCY` row with status `outside` and kept in the chain like any other row; it is never dropped, skipped or retried until it passes (the verifier's `DIAG` and `VERDICT` checks recompute the status from the stored differences).

**Recorded result before the scheduled runs: a miss.** The one real day tried, 2026-11-13 (dry run of 2026-11-19, local laptop, measured), gave RMSE 17.36 bp, largest 25.71 bp at 22 years: `outside` the 15 bp RMSE tolerance that was fixed beforehand. It is recorded as a miss of the consistency check on the one day tried. It is a consistency check, not proof: it neither shows the job wrong nor would a pass have shown it right. The fit weights (KR-98) were changed once, after one real day had been seen (worked example above); the tolerance and the method have not been changed since, and will not be changed to turn this result into a pass.

Timing: the Fed posts weekly, days behind [FRB-NYC]. A day's comparison stays pending (no row) until the Fed file has that date. It becomes `unavailable` (one row, with the reason) if the Fed file has later dates but not this one, or after 30 calendar days with no Fed row. A fetch failure of the Fed file writes nothing; the next run tries again.

## Row format (KR-102)

Format string `qf-curve-daily/1`. JSON Lines, file `ledger/curves.jsonl` on branch `curve-daily`; `ledger/summary.json` beside it is rewritten by each run (latest fit, counts, head).

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `seq` | integer from 0 |
| `ts` | UTC time of the run that wrote the row, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ` |
| `kind` | `GENESIS` (row 0 only), `DAY`, `GAP`, `CONSISTENCY` |
| `format`, `label` | as above |
| `body` | below |
| `prev`, `hash` | SHA-256 of the previous row (64 zeros for row 0) and of this row's canonical bytes |

Canonical bytes: the row without `hash`, keys sorted at every level, separators `,` and `:`, ASCII; every scalar a string except `seq` (the HR-45 rule of `10-execution/paper-trading.md`, as the paper ledger uses it). A line that repeats a key does not parse. Numbers from the CLI are written as the shortest decimal string that reads back to the same binary64 value. The file has one serialisation too (KR-208): each line is the row, hash included, written the same way and ended by one line feed, and the verifier fails (`BYTES`) the same rows in any other bytes. `ts` is exactly `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ`. The pinned rows and hashes are in `tools/paper_daily/canonical_selftest.py`, which CI job `python-daily-jobs-bytes` runs on the runner image that writes the real rows.

- `GENESIS`: `config` (all of `config.json`), `config_sha256`. The verifier fails if `config.json` on `main` later differs. Any change of tenors, fit, grid, weights, thresholds, schedule rule or row fields needs a new format string, a ruling and a new ledger; rows of version 1 are never rewritten.
- `DAY`: `date`, `timing` (`on_time` or `late`: the writing run's own label, from its clock), `status`, `run` (`commit`: the commit of `main` that was checked out and run, KR-214; `contract_version` from the CLI's `version` verb; `impl`). By status:
  - `ok`: `input` (`url`, `sha256` and `bytes` of the downloaded file, `latest_date` in it, `row_date`, `row_sha256` of the day's thirteen values as one text line, `attribution`) and `fit` (`operation`, `weighting`, `tau`, `betas`, `objective`, `taus_ok`, `taus_failed`, `errors_bp` per tenor, `rmse_bp`, `max_abs_bp`, `max_at`, `within_fit_flag`, `zeros_pct` for 1 to 30 years);
  - `fit_failed`: `error` (`code`, `detail`) and `input` as far as it was read; never a `fit`;
  - `stale_input`: `input` with `latest_date` before `date` (the file had not been updated);
  - `fetch_failed`: `input.url`; when bytes arrived that are not the file, also `input.sha256`, `input.bytes` and `error` (`E_NOT_THE_FILE`, detail);
  - `holdout_blocked`: `studies` (the prereg ids whose window holds `date`); no input is read.
- `GAP`: `date`, `cause` (`late_window` or `not_published`), `reason`, `run`, and for `not_published` the `input` that shows it (`url`, `sha256`, `bytes`, `latest_date` after `date`, `attribution`).
- `CONSISTENCY`: `date`, `day_row` (hash of the DAY row), `label`, `gsw` (`url`, `sha256`, `bytes`, `last_date`, `fetched_at`, `attribution`), `tolerance_bp`, `status` (`within`, `outside`, `unavailable`), and for the first two `diffs_bp`, `rmse_bp`, `max_abs_bp`, `max_at`; for `unavailable` a `reason`.

## When a day is due, on time, late or a gap (KR-94 to KR-96, KR-203, KR-206)

Plain idea: the job walks the business days strictly in order, one row or more per day, and never moves past a day it has not settled. That is what keeps a late or missing publication from breaking the chain.

- **Due**: business day `D` is due from 23:00:00 UTC on `D` (6:00 PM Eastern in winter, 7:00 PM in summer; Treasury usually posts by 6:00 PM ET [TY-M]).
- **On time**: a DAY row written before 19:30:00 UTC on the next business day (strictly before; a row written at 19:30:00 exactly is late; KR-111), before the next day's 3:30 PM ET input quotes in either daylight regime [UST-IRS]. Otherwise `late`. The label is the writing run's clock and the verifier checks it against `ts`. Outside evidence of the time is the anchor's Bitcoin block (KR-201): `verify_chain.py verify --anchors A --bitcoin-headers H` lists, for every row labelled on time, whether a block time plus 7,200 s before the cut-off covers it.
- **Settled**: a date is settled by one DAY row with status `ok`, `fit_failed` or `holdout_blocked`, or by a GAP row. `stale_input` and `fetch_failed` rows do not settle a date; later runs try again, and each try is a row.
- **Strict order** (KR-203): a run handles the due dates oldest first and **stops at the first date it cannot settle**. So at most one date is unsettled, it is the last date that has a row, and no later date gets a row before it is settled. Nothing is lost by stopping: if the file has not reached `D` it has not reached any later date, and if the fetch failed there is no file. The verifier holds every ledger to the same rule (`ORDER`, `DUP`, `MISSING`).
- **A date the source skips**: if the Treasury file holds a later date and not `D` (at a year end, if the next year's file holds any date), `D` is closed at once by a GAP row with `cause` "not_published" that carries that file's URL, hash and latest date, and the run goes on to the next date. `D` does not hold up the days after it.
- **Late window**: a run may still fit an unsettled date, labelled `late`, until 19:30 UTC on the fifth business day after the date's next business day. After that the run writes a GAP row with `cause` "late_window". **A gap is recorded as a gap and never filled later.**
- **Start**: the first run writes GENESIS and a DAY row for the latest due day only. Nothing is back-filled, and the verifier checks it (`START`).
- **Holdout** (plan 14.10 rule 6): before any input is read, each due date is checked against every prereg holdout window; a date inside one gets a `holdout_blocked` row and nothing else. Today every window ends before the first possible run date, so this rule is expected never to fire; it is there for future preregs.
- A run that finds nothing due writes nothing: no row, no commit, no anchor (KR-205). A run whose clock is before the last row's `ts` refuses.

Worked example (synthetic, `test_unsettled_date_followed_by_a_later_date_does_not_stop_the_chain`; this is the sequence audit C found). Day 1 fits. Day 2 is not in the file on the evening run, nor the next morning: two `stale_input` rows for day 2 and nothing for any other date. On the evening of day 3 the file holds days 1 and 3: the run writes a `not_published` GAP for day 2, then the fit of day 3, and the ledger verifies after this and every later run. In version 0.1.0 the third run wrote a stale row for day 2 and then a fit for day 3, and the fourth run's retry of day 2 failed `ORDER` for good.

**When the job runs** (KR-206). Measured from GitHub's start times of this repository's 24 scheduled runs of `recorder.yml` and `daily-workflows.yml` from 2026-11-09 to 2026-11-19 (`gh run list`, read 2026-11-19): the 23:30 UTC slot started 3.41 to 5.67 h after its cron time (n = 8), the 14:30 UTC slot 6.44 to 9.00 h (n = 8), the 15:00 UTC slot 6.24 to 8.73 h (n = 8); none was dropped. The second slot of version 0.1.0 (14:15 UTC) would have started between 20:40 and 23:15 UTC, after the cut-off, on every one of those days, so it could only write late rows. The slots to be restored are 23:45 UTC Monday to Friday and 03:30 and 06:30 UTC Tuesday to Saturday, which start between 03:10 and 15:30 UTC under every delay measured. Ten days of measurements are not a guarantee; a day that is late is labelled late.

## Failures (KR-99, KR-100)

A failed fit is a row with its error code (the CLI's code, for example `E_DOMAIN` or `E_SINGULAR`, or the job's own `E_MISSING_TENOR`, `E_PARSE`, `E_NONFINITE`, `E_PROBE_MISMATCH`), never a dropped day. A non-finite number anywhere in a fit is `E_NONFINITE`, a settled failed fit; in version 0.1.0 it was a refusal, which the same input would have repeated on every later run (KR-204). `run_day.py` exits 2 when it wrote any row with a problem (GAP, `fit_failed`, `stale_input`, `fetch_failed`, `holdout_blocked`); the workflow still pushes the rows and then turns the run red, so GitHub notifies the owner. Exit 1 is a refusal: the existing ledger does not verify, the new one would not, or the clock is before the last row; nothing is written, and the next run is not affected.

## Chain, anchor and verification (KR-103 to KR-105, KR-201, KR-203, KR-205, KR-207)

The chain is the `prev`/`hash` link. After a push that added rows the workflow writes `anchors/<UTC time>.json` (`count`, `head`, `format`, `config_sha256`) on branch `curve-daily-anchors` and stamps it with OpenTimestamps, as `recorder.yml` anchors the forward log (plan 13.2 R4). A head is stamped once; a head whose stamp failed is stamped by the next run; older proofs are upgraded on each run so that their Bitcoin attestations reach the branch. Before each push every anchor held must still match line `count` of the ledger. How an anchor's time is read from its proof and a Bitcoin block header is in `paper-ledger-daily.md`, "Chain and anchor" (one rule for both jobs, KR-201).

`tools/curve_daily/verify_chain.py verify` stops at the first failure and never repairs:

`verify_chain.py rebuild --date D --treasury-file F` refits one day from a saved copy of the Treasury file whose SHA-256 matches the row. It compares `tau`, `taus_ok`, `max_at`, `within_fit_flag`, `operation` and `weighting` exactly and every number within 1e-6 relative to max(1, value), and reports `exact` true or false (KR-207; `test_fit_is_deterministic_and_rebuilds`, `test_rebuild_allows_last_bit_noise_and_nothing_more`). The allowance exists because fit values are stored at full binary64 precision and pass through `exp`, `log` and a least-squares solve, so a refit on another platform or library build may differ in the last bits; on the same platform and build the refit is exact. The chain's own checks do not need it: `DIAG` and `VERDICT` recompute from the stored strings.

**The state machine as a property.** `python/tests/test_daily_jobs_properties.py` drives the writer through 10,000 generated sequences of runs (file on time, stale, never published for a date, failed fetch, a download that is not the file, a bad cell, missed runs, reruns at the same instant, two runs a day, outages of many days, a year end, a clock going backwards, a holdout window). After every run it asserts that the ledger only grew and verifies with the calendar; that every business day from the first to the last date has a row, in order, with at most the last one unsettled; that nothing was written before it was due, fitted after its late window or started earlier than the day due at the start; that every settled outcome is true of the simulated source; and that the run left nothing undone that it could do. The test is shown failing on a writer that goes on past a stale date (plan 14.10 rule 1). The calendar checks need the CLI; `--no-calendar` skips them and says so in its output.

## Limits

- **Audit findings of 2026-11-19 (KA-C9, Critical; KA-C5, KA-C10, KA-C11, KA-C12) and the job's schedule.** They are answered in this version of the spec and by rulings KR-203, KR-204, KR-206, KR-209 and KR-214; those rulings are proposed, not confirmed. The job is not scheduled until the coordinator confirms them and the Linux bytes job is green. Register: `docs/known-issues.md`.

- Three-factor Nelson-Siegel only. On both real days tried the fit RMSE was just above 10 bp and the residuals wave with the curve's two bends; a Svensson (four-factor) fit would follow them better, but the library has no Svensson fit operation (it has the Svensson formula only in `tools/validation/cb_consistency.py`, outside the library). No par bootstrap of the Treasury curve exists in the library either (`rates.ois.bootstrap` is for swap quotes). Both are listed for another lane in the handover; this job does not add them.
- Par bonds are a convention: settlement on `D`, no T+1, no day-count difference between bills and coupon securities beyond what `bond.price.from_yield` does, and Treasury's own curve method (monotone convex [TY-M]) is not reproduced.
- Zero rates are compared at `D + n` years on the ACT/365 axis of `bonds.md` against the Fed's values at `n` years; the difference in time is under one day per year of maturity, well under 0.1 bp on these curves (inferred).
- The Fed curve is a staff research product that may be revised or delayed [FRB-NYC]; a comparison uses the file as fetched that day, identified by its SHA-256.
- No raw file is kept anywhere by the scheduled job. A third party can check the chain and the arithmetic of every row, and can recover the thirteen par yields a row used from the row itself (see "What is fetched and what is published"); a full refit with the rebuild command needs their own copy of the Treasury file whose hash matches (Treasury may later revise a value, which then shows as a hash mismatch).
- The `timing` label is the runner's clock. Outside evidence of when a row existed is its anchor's Bitcoin block, read with block headers the verifier supplies; the header's proof of work and a difficulty floor are checked, not its place in the best chain (`paper-ledger-daily.md`, Limits).
- The live dry runs quoted above were made with version 0.1.0 of the tools. The fit is unchanged since; the row fields, the schedule rule and the verifier changed in version 0.2.0, and no dry run against the live files has been made with it by this lane.
- Python CLI only; the other three builds are not run by this job.
- GitHub may delay scheduled runs and disables schedules after 60 days without repository activity; the late window and the GAP rule record what that costs.
