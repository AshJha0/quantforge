# Research workflow: from backtest to PBO

<p class="note">Status: Implemented, not Validated. Validation is self-assessment until the human review at the end of the project. Data: synthetic. These are constructed inputs, not market data, and nothing is traded.</p>

Generated on 2026-11-14 from commit `784ab37`, by Java 0.8.3 (JDK 25.0.5+8-LTS), C++ 0.8.3 (GCC 16.2.0), Python 0.8.3 (CPython 3.15.2), Rust 0.8.3 (rustc 1.99.1), on one Windows machine.

<p class="note">A teaching example, not a trading claim. The prices are a seeded random walk with no drift, so no rule on this page has an edge: whatever looks good here is luck, and the point of the page is to show the statistics saying so.</p>

## What it is

A research study tries several rules, keeps the best and reports its Sharpe ratio. This page walks one such study through the stage J operations: twelve rules backtested with costs, the winner's probabilistic and deflated Sharpe ratios, the winner chosen inside purged cross-validation folds and scored outside them, and the probability of backtest overfitting across all twelve. Stage J, released in v0.9.0 (`specifications/11-research/`).

Formally, the deflated Sharpe ratio is the probability that the true Sharpe exceeds the one the best of `N` useless trials would show, `SR0`, and PBO is the share of training and test splits in which the training winner ranks in the bottom half out of sample. [Tutorial 23](../getting-started/tutorial-23-backtest-honestly.md) and [Tutorial 24](../getting-started/tutorial-24-is-the-sharpe-real.md) explain each step on hand-sized data; [LEARN, stage J](../../LEARN.md#stage-j-backtest-overfitting-and-research-statistics) gives the derivations.

## Inputs

502 daily closes from [`examples/research_workflow_j.json`](../../examples/research_workflow_j.json): a log-normal random walk with 1 % daily volatility, no drift, seed 20261114, starting at 100, generated by `tools/gallery_j.py`. The repository holds no real daily price series long enough for this (the real series it records are a month of SOFR fixings and single-day curves), and none is downloaded here. Twelve rules: momentum, holding the sign of the change over the last 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 40, 60 or 100 days, and reversal, holding the opposite sign over 2, 5, 10 or 20 days. Every rule waits 100 bars, trades one unit with lag 1 and pays a half-spread of 1 bp. Statistics use the 400 bars from bar 102, where every rule can have P&L.

## Is the winner's Sharpe real?

The best rule in sample is momentum 60, annual Sharpe 1.043 (daily 0.06573) over 400 days.

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---|
| PSR against 0 | 0.9061 |
| Trials `N` | 12 |
| Variance of the trials' daily Sharpe | 0.00128 |
| `SR0`, daily (what the best of 12 useless rules shows) | 0.05956 |
| DSR | 0.5491 |

Read the two probabilities together: the PSR asks whether the winner beats zero, the DSR whether it beats the luck of choosing among 12. A DSR below 0.95 is not significant at 5 %.

## The winner chosen inside purged folds

5 contiguous folds over the 400 bars. Each observation's label is its bar's P&L, window `[i, i + 1]`, so the purge drops the neighbour on each side of a test block, and an embargo of 0.01 drops the 4 indices after it, the first of which the purge has already dropped. In each fold the rule with the best training Sharpe is picked and scored on the test block.

Averaged over the folds, the picked rule's annual Sharpe is 1.157 in training and 0.077 on the test blocks. The fall from one to the other is the selection bias the DSR allows for.

## Probability of backtest overfitting

CSCV on the 400 x 12 matrix of daily P&L in 8 blocks: 70 splits, Sharpe metric. PBO = 0.4571: in 32 of 70 splits the training winner landed at or below the median out of sample. On prices with no edge the winner's test rank is as likely to be low as high, so a PBO near one half is what noise gives; only a PBO well below one half would suggest the in-sample ranking carries over [BBLdPZ17].
