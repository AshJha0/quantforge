## 1. Large exposures, G-SIB limit (KR-133, KR-66)

**Ruling as written.** KR-66: "breach iff > 25% (or > 15% when both sides are flagged G-SIB)". KR-133: "A group takes the 15% limit when `bank_gsib` is true and **any** member is flagged G-SIB (inferred ...). The blind lane assumed 'every member'; no golden has a group with mixed flags, so no matched value depends on it."

**Text.**
- LEX20.1 (p. 943): "this figure is set at 15% for a global systemically important bank's (G-SIB's) exposures to another G-SIB." The limit is 25% for "a single counterparty or ... a group of connected counterparties".
- LEX40.1 (p. 958): "The large exposure limit applied to a global systemically important bank's (G-SIB's) exposure to another G-SIB is set at 15% of Tier 1 capital." LEX40.2 and 40.3 say nothing about groups; LEX40.3 only discusses consolidated versus legal-entity application.
- LEX10.9 (p. 940): a group of connected counterparties "must be treated as a single counterparty".

**Reading.** The text frames the 15% limit as G-SIB bank to G-SIB counterparty, so both sides matter (the `bank_gsib` input is right). It says a connected group is one counterparty, but never says whether a group is "another G-SIB" when only some members are G-SIBs. "Any member", "every member" and "the largest member" are all consistent with the words; the text does not choose.

**Verdict: text is genuinely silent or ambiguous** on mixed groups; it supports the two-sided framing.

**Golden impact.** KR-133 says no golden has a mixed group. `datasets/golden/stage_k/lex_k.json` lex_k-0009 does: group GP = {P (`gsib: true`), Q (no flag)}, `bank_gsib` true, Tier 1 400. The expected `groups[0].limit` is 60.0 (15%), which is the "any member" reading. Under an "every member" reading the limit would be 100.0 (25%); `share`, `large` and `breach` would not change (exposure 23). Name this case wherever KR-133 is cited. The "any member" reading is the cautious one (a supervisor would not want a G-SIB hidden inside a group to dodge the tighter limit) but that is an argument, not text.

## 2. SA-CVA counterparty credit spread buckets 1a and 1b (KR-121 (b))

**Ruling as written.** "MAR50.63 Table 5 prints bucket 1 as one bucket with two sub-sectors a) and b), and Table 6 has one row for bucket 1; only Table 7 splits the risk weight. So `1a` and `1b` select the risk weight and are aggregated as one bucket `1`."

**Text.**
- MAR50.63 (p. 741) Table 5: eight buckets, numbered 1 to 8; bucket 1 lists "a) Sovereigns including central banks, multilateral development banks" and "b) Local government, government-backed non-financials, education and public administration".
- MAR50.64 (p. 742) Table 6: cross-bucket correlations are an 8 by 8 matrix (rows 1 to 8), one row and one column for bucket 1: 10%, 20%, 25%, 20%, 15%, 0%, 45% against buckets 2 to 8.
- MAR50.65 (pp. 742-743) Table 7: risk weights have columns "1 a)", "1 b)", 2, ..., 8: IG 0.5%, 1.0%, 5.0%, 3.0%, 3.0%, 2.0%, 1.5%, 5.0%, 1.5%; HY and NR 2.0%, 4.0%, 12.0%, 7.0%, 8.5%, 5.5%, 5.0%, 12.0%, 5.0%.

**Verdict: text supports the ruling.** One bucket for aggregation, two risk weights. The Table 6 matrix has no 1a-1b entry, so no cross-bucket correlation applies between them; within the bucket the ρ_tenor x ρ_name x ρ_quality rule of MAR50.65(4) applies.

**Golden impact.** None. sacva_k-0010 (CCS bucket 1a and 1b names S1, M1, output bucket "1") already follows this reading.

## 3. SA-CVA equity vega risk weight 78% (KR-124)

**Ruling as written.** "MAR50.73(3) gives 78% to 'large market capitalisation buckets' without listing them. Table 11 prints size 'Large' for buckets 1 to 8 and 'Large cap, advanced economies' for the index bucket 12; buckets 9 and 10 (small), 11 (other sector) and 13 (other indices) take 100%."

**Text.**
- MAR50.73(3) (p. 748): "The risk weights for equity volatilities RWk are set to 78% for large market capitalisation buckets and to 100% for the other buckets."
- MAR50.70 Table 11 (pp. 746-747): buckets 1 to 4 size "Large", region emerging market economies; buckets 5 to 8 "Large", advanced economies; buckets 9 and 10 "Small"; bucket 11 "(Not applicable)", "Other sector"; bucket 12 "Large cap, advanced economies", "Qualified Indices"; bucket 13 "Other", "Qualified Indices". MAR50.70(2): large is market cap of USD 2 billion or more.

**Verdict: text supports the ruling.** The 78% buckets are those whose Table 11 size reads "Large" (1 to 8) plus bucket 12 whose size cell reads "Large cap". The only soft spot is bucket 12 (an index bucket); the table labels it large cap, so there is no better reading.

**Golden impact.** None.

## 4. MAR32.19 desk-level backtesting (KR-44)

**Ruling as written.** "Breach when the 99% count is more than 12, or the 97.5% count is more than 30. ... the other reading (30 itself a breach) would differ only at exactly 30. Goldens btk_k-0019 (12 and 30: eligible) and btk_k-0021 (31: breach) pin our reading."

**Text.** MAR32.19 (p. 662): "If any given trading desk experiences either more than 12 exceptions at the 99th percentile or 30 exceptions at the 97.5th percentile in the most recent 12-month period, the capital requirement for all of the positions in the trading desk must be determined using the standardised approach."

Context read: MAR32.16-32.18 (same page; the desk test, how exceptions are counted; its FAQ1 is about volatility scaling and does not touch the limits), footnote 307 (p. 663, two-stage approval), the bank-level table MAR32.9 (p. 660-661, which uses "10 or more" for the red zone, so the framework does have an "or more" idiom and chose "more than" here), and the PLA thresholds MAR32.42 (p. 667, "above 0.80", "below 0.09"). A search of the whole PDF for "97.5th" and "12 exceptions" found only this paragraph. No FAQ, footnote or other paragraph restates the 30.

**Verdict: text is genuinely silent or ambiguous.** Grammatically "more than" can govern both numbers ("more than 12 ... or [more than] 30") or only the first. The framework's own idiom for an inclusive limit ("10 or more") was not used, which weakly favours the strict reading, but weak is the right word.

**Golden impact if exactly 30 at 97.5% is a breach.** btk_k-0019 (`risk.frtb.backtest.desk`, observations 250, exceptions_99 12 and 10, exceptions_975 30 and 28): the 97.5% count is max(30, 28) = 30, expected result flips from eligible to breach (and its 99% count of 12 stays eligible). btk_k-0021 (31) and btk_k-0020 (13 at 99%) are unchanged. Any btk_k case with exactly 30 at 97.5% would flip; I did not scan every case, only btk_k-0019 to 0021 were read.

## 5. BA-CVA scalars and the spec formulas (cva-capital.md)

**Ruling as written (spec).** `K_reduced = sqrt((0.5 sum SCVA)^2 + 0.75 sum SCVA^2)`; `K_full = 0.25 K_reduced + 0.75 K_hedged`; capital = `0.65 K_reduced` or `0.65 K_full`; `m_cva` defaults to 1 (KR-63).

**Text.**
- MAR50.14 (pp. 725-726): "The capital requirements for CVA risk under the reduced version of the BA-CVA (DS_BA-CVA x K_reduced, where the discount scalar DS_BA-CVA = 0.65)"; "ρ = 50%"; "ρ^2 = 25%". Formula image p. 726: `K_reduced = sqrt((ρ sum SCVA_c)^2 + (1 - ρ^2) sum SCVA_c^2)`, that is 0.5 and 0.75. Same-page image: `SCVA_c = (1/α) RW_c sum M_NS EAD_NS DF_NS`, α = 1.4, DF = (1 - e^(-0.05 M)) / (0.05 M), 1 for IMM.
- MAR50.20 (p. 728): "DS_BA-CVA = 0.65, and β = 0.25 is the supervisory parameter" and image `K_full = β K_reduced + (1 - β) K_hedged`.
- MAR50.21 image (p. 729): `K_hedged = sqrt((ρ sum (SCVA - SNH) - IH)^2 + (1 - ρ^2) sum (SCVA - SNH)^2 + sum HMA)`; MAR50.23 image: `SNH_c = sum r_hc RW_h M_h B_h DF_h`; MAR50.24 image: `IH = sum RW_i M_i B_i DF_i`; MAR50.25 image: `HMA_c = sum (1 - r_hc^2) (RW_h M_h B_h DF_h)^2` (p. 730); MAR50.26 Table 2: r_hc 100%, 80%, 50%.
- MAR50.24(4): index RW is the Table 1 value times 0.7 (one sector and one quality), or the name-weighted average times 0.7 (mixed).
- m_CVA: MAR50.40 (p. 737): "Aggregated capital requirements can be scaled up by the multiplier mCVA"; MAR50.41: "The multiplier mCVA is set at 1." It appears in the SA-CVA formula `K = m_CVA sqrt(...)` (MAR50.53 image, p. 738). The SA-CVA section contains no discount scalar. A search of MAR50 for 0.65 and 1.25 found 0.65 only at MAR50.14 and MAR50.20.

**Verdict: text supports the spec.** Discount scalar 0.65 applies to BA-CVA only, to both K_reduced and K_full, outside the square roots; β = 0.25 enters only K_full (the reduced version has no β); ρ = 0.5 and (1 - ρ^2) = 0.75; m_CVA = 1 applies to SA-CVA only (the earlier 1.25 was wrong); a higher m_CVA is a supervisory add-on, which the spec treats as an input with `m_cva >= 1`.

**Golden impact.** None. The Basel text has no five-year cap (MAR50.15(2)) and no IMM DF for hedges, consistent with KR-64 and KR-122 (b).

## 6. FRTB IMA liquidity-horizon cascade (MAR33.4 to MAR33.6; KR-58, KR-122 (a))

**KR-58 as written.** "MAR33.6(2) text floors ES_F,C / ES_R,C at 1; the image on p. 672 and the IMCC image on p. 676 omit it. We apply the floor to the portfolio ratio and to each risk-class ratio."

**Text.** MAR33.6 (p. 672): "ES_R,S is then scaled up by the ratio of (i) the current ES using the full set of risk factors to (ii) the current ES measure using the reduced set of factors. For the purpose of this calculation, this ratio is floored at 1." The formula image directly below reads `ES = ES_R,S x ES_F,C / ES_R,C` with no max. Same paragraph defines ES_F,C and ES_R,C.

**Verdict: text supports KR-58** (the paragraph states the floor; the image omits it; the text wins). The text speaks of "this ratio" for the portfolio ES; applying it per risk class in the IMCC (MAR33.15) is an extension the text does not state either way (I did not re-read the IMCC image in this lane).

**KR-122 (a) as written.** A single nonzero ES_j gives `ES_j sqrt(w_j)` with w = (1, 1, 2, 2, 6); the "sum of weights" form holds when every risk factor has liquidity horizon LH_j, so that ES_1 = ... = ES_j = E and ES = E sqrt(LH_j / 10).

**Text.** MAR33.4 image (p. 671): `ES = sqrt( ES_T(P)^2 + sum_{j>=2} ( ES_T(P,j) sqrt((LH_j - LH_{j-1}) / T) )^2 )`, T = 10, LH = 10, 20, 40, 60, 120 (Table 1, p. 671). So the weights are 1 (for ES_T(P), j = 1), then 1, 2, 2, 6 for j = 2 to 5. MAR33.4(6): "Q(pi, j) is a subset of Q(pi, j-1)".

**Verdict: text supports KR-122 (a).** A lone ES_j with j >= 2 gives ES_j sqrt(w_j); with ES_1 through ES_j all equal to E the radicand is E^2 (1 + 1 + ... ) = E^2 LH_j / 10 (j = 5 gives E sqrt(12), the es_k-0002 case), because the weights telescope to (LH_j - 10)/10 plus the leading 1.

**Golden impact.** None.

## 7. DRC (MAR22; KR-52, KR-53, KR-54)

**Maturity weighting (KR-52).** Ruling: `w = 1` if maturity >= 1, else `max(maturity, 0.25)`, applied to gross JTD before offsetting; cash equity maturity must be 0.25 or at least 1.
- MAR22.15 (p. 624): JTD of exposures "of maturity less than one year ... are scaled by a fraction of a year. No scaling is applied to the JTD for exposures of one year or greater". Footnote 296: the paragraph "refers to the scaling of gross JTD (ie not net JTD)".
- MAR22.18 (p. 626): the weighting for any product with maturity under three months "is floored at a weighting factor of one-fourth".
- MAR22.16 (p. 625): cash equity "assigned to a maturity of either more than one year or three months".
- MAR22.20 (p. 627): when both legs have maturity under one year "the scaling can be applied to both".

**Verdict: text supports KR-52.** One small difference: for cash equity the text says "more than one year", the ruling accepts exactly 1 (weight 1 either way). I did not scan the DRC goldens for a cash equity at exactly 1.0.

**Offsetting by seniority (KR-53, and the deriver's noted ambiguity on direction).** Ruling: "A short may offset a long of equal or higher rank"; shorts served in descending rank, each using longs of its rank or above.
- MAR22.19(1) (p. 626): "The gross JTD risk positions of long and short exposures to the same obligor may be offset where the short exposure has the same or lower seniority relative to the long exposure. For example, a short exposure in an equity may offset a long exposure in a bond, but a short exposure in a bond cannot offset a long exposure in the equity."
- LEX30.23 (p. 948) says the same for the large exposures framework: offset "only when the short position is junior to the long position, or if the positions are of the same seniority".

**Verdict: text supports KR-53, and the direction is not ambiguous.** Short rank <= long rank; the equity-short against bond-long example is explicit. (The greedy processing order inside that rule is not in the text; it is a tie-resolving ruling and gives the maximal offset because the eligible sets are nested.) Covered bonds: MAR22.12(3) gives an LGD of 25% and no seniority; ranking them above senior debt stays a ruling, the text is silent.

**Hedge benefit ratio.** MAR22.23 (p. 627): HBR is "the ratio of net long JTD risk positions to the sum of net long JTD and absolute value of net short JTD risk positions", sums taken unweighted across rating bands. MAR22.25 image (p. 628): `DRC_b = max[ (sum_long RW_i net JTD_i) - HBR (sum_short RW_i |net JTD_i|); 0 ]`, no cross-bucket hedging (MAR22.26), total is the simple sum of buckets.

**Verdict: text supports the spec's HBR and DRC_b.** KR-54 (HBR undefined when both sums are 0, reported as null with DRC 0; only shorts gives HBR 0): **text is silent**; the ruling is a convention and stays one.

**Bucket weights by credit quality.** MAR22.24 Table 2 (pp. 627-628): AAA 0.5%, AA 2%, A 3%, BBB 6%, BB 15%, B 30%, CCC 50%, Unrated 15%, Defaulted 100%, for all three buckets (corporates, sovereigns, local governments). **Text supports** `specifications/tables/stage_k/mar22_drc.json` (entries rw.AAA ... rw.Defaulted checked by name; values match the printed table as extracted).

**Golden impact.** None.

## 8. Other rulings the text speaks to (KR-50 to KR-69, KR-112 to KR-135)

Ordered by consequence. Each is a place a ruling or note calls a point inferred, assumed, chosen or "not read", and the text does speak. None contradicts a golden's expected value as far as the cases were checked; "tighten" means a spec or ruling wording could cite the paragraph.

1. **KR-133 / lex_k-0009**: see item 1; the "no mixed group" statement is wrong and should be corrected in the ruling text.
2. **KR-55 (securitisation risk weights)**: the ruling says CRE40-CRE44 was not read. They are in the same PDF (CRE40 from p. 348, CRE41 p. 392, CRE43 p. 404, CRE44 p. 408 by first occurrence). MAR22.34(1) and MAR22.42 (pp. 630, 632-633) say the weights are the banking book securitisation weights "with the following modification: the maturity component ... is set to zero (ie a one-year maturity is assumed)", the hierarchy of approaches is applied at the underlying pool level (MAR22.34(2)), and the capital for a cash position can be capped at its fair value (MAR22.34(3)). So the caller-supplied `rw` stays a scope choice, not a gap in the text; the caller's rw must be computed with maturity 1 year. Tighten the KR-55 label.
3. **KR-66 and KR-131 (LEX trading book)**: supported. LEX30.17 (p. 948): gross JTD "without application of risk weighting with the exception that all instruments must be assigned a loss-given-default of 100%"; LEX30.18: "The maturity adjustments set out in MAR22.15 to MAR22.18 are not applicable". LEX30.29 (p. 949): netting across banking and trading books is not permitted; LEX30.30: a net short with one counterparty "need not be considered" as an exposure.
4. **KR-132 and KR-134 (group exposure)**: LEX30.19 (p. 948): the trading book exposure to a group "is the sum of positive (ie net long) gross jump-to-defaults for each counterparty within that group". This says per-counterparty netting then a sum of positive nets, which is what KR-132 plus the group sum does; a net short of one member must not reduce another member's long. Worth citing. LEX30.26 (p. 949) lets a bank opt for no offsetting across different issues (not modelled; the operation always applies LEX30.22-30.23 netting).
5. **KR-51 (ratings per obligor)**: MAR22.24 FAQ1 (p. 628) states a rule for several ratings: if two ratings map to different risk weights "the higher risk weight should be applied"; with three or more, take the two lowest risk weights and apply the higher of those two. KR-51 says ratings are per obligor and the caller supplies them. The text speaks to how the caller picks; add a note in the spec.
6. **KR-115 (one rw per netting group; offsetting limited to same pool and same tranche)**: supported by MAR22.29 (p. 629) "no offsetting ... with different underlying securitised portfolio ... [or] from different tranches with the same securitised portfolio", and MAR22.30 (maturity differences may be offset, MAR22.15-22.18 scaling applies). KR-55's omission of replication decomposition (MAR22.28, MAR22.30(1)-(2)) is consistent with the text allowing it as an option ("may"), not requiring it.
7. **KR-59 (RFET lag)**: "we do not model a lag". MAR31.13 footnote 301 and FAQ1 (pp. 650-651) allow a one-year period ending up to one month before the assessment; "may", so not modelling it is permitted. The text also says the 24-observation criterion is measured "over the period used to calibrate the current ES model" and the 100-observation criterion "over the previous 12 months" (MAR31.13(1)-(2), p. 650); the ruling uses 12 months for both, which is the lag-free case. The Table 1 row (D) bucket last interval is printed "0.95 <= delta < 1.00" (p. 652), so delta = 1 outside the table is also the text's literal reading.
8. **KR-60 (liquidity horizon)**: the FAQ4 and FAQ5 rules (p. 675) are the ruling's source and are followed. Two soft points. FAQ4 says "the next longer liquidity horizon length compared with the maturity of the instrument", while MAR33.12(3) (p. 674) says horizons "should be capped at the maturity"; the ruling picks the smallest of the five horizons at or above the maturity (a maturity of exactly 20 days gives 20, not 40), the reading that fits the cap sentence. The text is silent on equality. Footnote 309 says "first-order crosses across these specified currency pairs are also subject to the same liquidity horizon", which the ruling's common-third-currency rule implements; the footnote does not define the cross precisely.
9. **KR-56 (PLA)**: supported. MAR32.42 (p. 667): green iff "above 0.80" and "below 0.09"; red iff "less than 0.7" or "above 0.12"; amber otherwise, so equality at any threshold is amber exactly as KR-56 rules. MAR32.36 (p. 666) says ranks are "the lowest value ... receives a rank of 1, the next lowest ... 2" and nothing on ties, as the ruling states. MAR32.39-32.41: ECDF step 0.004, KS the largest absolute difference.
10. **KR-61 and KR-62 (IMA multiplier; SA-CVA roots)**: KR-61 is supported: MAR33.42 (p. 685) fixes m_c at 1.5 plus the backtesting "plus" of 0 to 0.5 and a qualitative add-on; MAR32.9 Table 1 (p. 661) gives 1.50 to 2.00 with "10 or more" at 2.00; "maximum of the exceptions ... against APL and HPL" (MAR33.42(4)). KR-62 is confirmed as a pure ruling: MAR50.53 (p. 738) has no floor under either root, so the text is silent and the ruling is a convention.

Also checked and consistent, no action: KR-120 (b) IR delta factors (MAR50.56-50.57, pp. 738-739: five tenors plus inflation for the reporting currency and USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, CAD, SEK, JPY; one parallel curve factor and inflation at 1.58% for others), KR-125 (b) RCS gamma (MAR50.67 Table 9 and "divided by 2" across credit quality for buckets 1 to 14, p. 745), KR-64 (no five-year cap, DF 1 under IMM, MAR50.15).
